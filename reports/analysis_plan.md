# Analysis plan

## Scope

This undergraduate project studies associations between national life expectancy and socioeconomic, healthcare, and public-health measures in country-year panel data. The present phase covers repository setup and data auditing only; no final regression model has been estimated.

## Observed dataset facts

- The raw CSV has 2,938 observations and 22 variables after trimming whitespace from column headers in memory.
- It covers 193 countries and the years 2000–2015.
- `Country` and `Year` uniquely identify every row; there are no exact duplicate rows.
- The panel is unbalanced. 183 countries have 16 observations, while 10 countries have one observation each.
- Missingness is concentrated in `Population` (652; 22.192%), `Hepatitis B` (553; 18.822%), `GDP` (448; 15.248%), and `Total expenditure` (226; 7.692%). Full results are generated by `src/data_audit.py`.
- Simple whitespace/case normalization found no inconsistent country-name variants. This is not a geopolitical or source-level entity validation.
- Several counts and scale variables are strongly skewed. Abrupt within-country jumps also require source validation before modeling.

## Modeling decisions

The primary outcome is `Life expectancy`. The first defensible associational model, after cleaning and missing-data review, is:

`Life expectancy_it = country FE + year FE + Schooling_it + log(GDP per capita_it) + Total expenditure_it + Polio_it + HIV/AIDS_it + error_it`

Standard errors should be clustered by country. This specification is deliberately parsimonious and is a starting point, not a final model.

The primary model should exclude `Adult Mortality`, `infant deaths`, and `under-five deaths`, because these are mortality outcomes or components mechanically close to life expectancy. It should not automatically combine all vaccination measures or all development/expenditure measures because of likely collinearity and overlapping definitions. `Status` will normally be absorbed by country fixed effects if it is time-invariant.

## Planned workflow

1. Verify source definitions, units, and country coverage.
2. Resolve suspicious values and document every correction without altering the raw CSV.
3. Create a processed dataset through a reproducible cleaning script.
4. Assess missingness patterns and compare complete-case samples with defensible alternatives.
5. Summarize within- and between-country variation.
6. Produce exploratory figures without interpreting them causally.
7. Estimate pooled OLS only as a descriptive benchmark.
8. Estimate country and two-way fixed-effects models with country-clustered standard errors.
9. Conduct sensitivity checks for variable choice, transformations, missing data, and influential observations.
10. Write the final report with a clear separation between association and causation.

## Questions requiring human review

- Is `GDP` measured per capita and in current US dollars, and are there known decimal or digit-loss issues?
- What exactly do `percentage expenditure`, `Total expenditure`, and `HIV/AIDS` measure?
- Should `Income composition of resources` replace rather than accompany schooling and GDP?
- Are zeros in development and expenditure variables genuine zeros or missing-value codes?
- Should the ten single-year countries be excluded from fixed-effects estimation?
- What missing-data strategy is substantively defensible for the intended research question?
